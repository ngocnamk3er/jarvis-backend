# Research report

Use this once the searching is done, to turn findings into the answer.

## Before writing
- Put every claim you intend to make next to the page it came from. A claim with no page behind it is left out or marked as your own inference.
- When two sources disagree, say so and give both figures with their sources. Do not pick one silently.
- Note the date or version a figure refers to. Software versions, prices and benchmarks go stale; "PostgreSQL 17 (Sep 2024)" beats "PostgreSQL".

## Shape
1. **Bottom line** — two or three sentences that answer the question as asked. Someone who reads only this should not be misled.
2. **Comparison table** when there are two or more options and more than one dimension. One row per dimension, one column per option; keep cells short and put the nuance in the text below.
3. **Per-topic notes** — what the table cannot hold: trade-offs, conditions under which the ranking flips, caveats.
4. **Sources** — a list of the pages used, each with what it supported.

## Rules
- Numbers carry units and a source; a benchmark carries what was measured and on what.
- Say what you could not find. "No independent benchmark found" is a finding.
- Do not pad. If the answer fits in a paragraph, write a paragraph.
